Capturing effect size heterogeneity is a key challenge in meta-analysis.
The common assumption that heterogeneity is normally distributed often falls short; sometimes, because the population is actually composed of multiple unobserved subgroups.
Finite mixture models can capture these unobserved sources of heterogeneity.
I introduce and validate open source software for estimating random-effects mixture, or "remix" meta-analyses.
Implemented in `OpenMx`, these structural equation models can be fully customized and further extended.
Results show that the method is fast and has low bias, but confidence interval coverage is below nominal levels,
and class enumeration remains challenging - which are known limitations of LCA.
